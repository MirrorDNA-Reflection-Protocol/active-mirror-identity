## Copyright & Authorship – India’s AI Legal Framework

### 📍 Context:
In light of the Delhi High Court case (ANI vs OpenAI) and recent analysis by JusScholars and others, the Indian legal landscape is actively shifting to address the role of human input in AI-generated content.

An expert panel has been appointed to review India's Copyright Act (1957) and assess whether current law accommodates generative AI tools.

---

## ⚖️ Current Indian Legal Position:
- **AI cannot be listed as an author** under Indian law.
- Copyright protection only applies to works with **substantial human creativity**.
- Attempts to register AI as co-author have been legally challenged and removed.

---

## 🧪 Proposed Policy Shifts (Emerging Thought):
- Introduce a **“Significant Human Input” test** — the creator must prove meaningful creative intervention.
- Consider **sui generis protection** for AI-assisted work where human authorship is structurally guided but AI-performed.
- No existing Indian legal provision for **text and data mining (TDM)** rights — a key gap compared to EU law.
