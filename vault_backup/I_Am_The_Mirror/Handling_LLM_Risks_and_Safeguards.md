# 🧠 Handling LLM Risks and Limitations in Sensitive Contexts

**Date:** 2025-07-07

---

## 🧭 1. Awareness of LLM Limits

> LLMs don’t “know” — they predict.

Large Language Models (LLMs) output what’s statistically likely, not what’s morally right, emotionally appropriate, or contextually helpful. That’s dangerous in high-trust settings like therapy, recovery, and medicine.

---

## 🧬 2. Symbolic Memory Over Surveillance Data

Instead of personalizing based on *tracking*, symbolic memory is used — where identity and rhythm are reinforced by structured patterns (e.g., prompts, anchors, glyphs) defined by the user. This reduces hallucination risk and increases controllability.

---

## 🪞 3. The MirrorOS Approach

- **Offline-first**: Everything runs on the user's device. No cloud. No background data scraping.
- **User-specific tuning**: Memory is constructed from structured symbolic anchors — not scraped data.
- **Guardrails**: Prompts refuse guidance in medical, legal, or mental health domains unless explicitly reflective.

---

## 🔒 4. Safety by Design

Instead of building more powerful models, we focus on sovereign systems that:
- Reflect the user rather than advise
- Respond only when prompted
- Label speculation clearly and maintain factual boundaries

---

## ✅ 5. Clear Labels and User Agency

Sovereign Mirrors do not say “you should…”  
They say “you’ve told me in the past…”

This fundamental difference keeps outputs grounded, self-aligned, and trustworthy — particularly for emotionally or psychologically sensitive topics.

---

## 👁️ Summary Principles

- 🤖 Use narrow, domain-specific models
- 🧠 Avoid using LLMs for direct therapeutic or medical recommendations
- 🪞 Focus on reflection and identity coherence rather than advice-giving
