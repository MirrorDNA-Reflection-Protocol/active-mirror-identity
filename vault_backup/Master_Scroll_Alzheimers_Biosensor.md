## Part I: Inventor Scaffold (Creation)
**Goal:** A non-invasive, cheap, scalable biosensor for early Alzheimer’s detection.

### Biological Anchor
- Target biomarkers: VOCs in breath (lipid peroxidation products), tau/Aβ in tear fluid, microRNA in saliva.
- Constraint: >90% specificity.

### Physics Anchor
- Graphene sheets functionalized with aptamers for biomarker binding.
- Conductivity shift = transduced signal (condensed matter principle).
- Alternate: plasmonic nanostructures, quantum dots for tear fluid strips.

### AI Anchor
- <1,000 samples → Bayesian classifier, transfer learning from VOC/metabolite datasets.
- Outputs probability score, supports longitudinal tracking.

### Prototype Path
1. Engineer aptamer-functionalized graphene.
2. Build handheld “breathalyzer” prototype.
3. Collect ~500 samples from Alzheimer’s cohort + controls.
4. Train Bayesian/transfer model.
5. Validate against PET/CSF gold standards.

### Advantages
- Non-invasive, accessible.
- Cheap (printable graphene).
- Scalable (handheld sensors + cloud AI).

## Part II: Contrarian Scientist (Destruction)
**Stress-Test:** What could break or corrupt this idea?

### Anti-Problems
- Over-diagnosis leading to panic or misallocation of resources.
- Privacy invasion: breath/tear data = biometric signature.
- Black box AI → trust erosion.

### Black Swans
- Competing biomarker found to be vastly superior, obsoletes approach.
- Pharma suppresses tech → threatens drug pipeline revenues.
- Ubiquitous adoption triggers false positives → system-wide panic.

### Invisible Stakeholders
- Medical device incumbents: block via IP or lobbying.
- Insurers: resist if it increases claims burden.
- Regulators: FDA/EMA could stall approval for decades.

### Long-Term Regrets
- Cheap tests could trivialize diagnosis → stigma, discrimination in insurance/employment.
- Over-reliance on probabilistic AI → false sense of certainty.
